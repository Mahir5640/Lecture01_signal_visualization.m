# Lecture 01: Signal Visualization and Analysis

## Task 2: Frequency Comparison
- **Which signal changes fastest?**  
  The **10 Hz** signal changes the fastest because it undergoes the highest number of oscillations per second.
- **Which signal has the lowest frequency?**  
  The **2 Hz** signal has the lowest frequency.
- **How can you see the difference in the plots?**  
  Within the 1-second window, the 2 Hz signal completes only 2 complete cycles, the 5 Hz signal completes 5 cycles, and the 10 Hz signal completes 10 cycles. The peaks and troughs are packed much closer together as frequency increases.

## Task 3: Amplitude Comparison
- **Which signal has the largest amplitude?**  
  The signal with **Amplitude = 2.0** has the largest amplitude (oscillating between +2 and -2).
- **Does changing amplitude change frequency?**  
  **No.** Changing amplitude only scales the vertical height (intensity/magnitude) of the wave; the number of cycles per second remains unchanged.
- **Real-world example where amplitude is important:**  
  **Audio volume control.** In acoustic signals, wave amplitude corresponds directly to perceived loudness. Increasing audio volume increases the signal's electrical amplitude to the speakers without changing musical pitch (frequency).

---

## Task 4: Noise Comparison
- **What changed after adding noise?**  
  The smooth, continuous sinusoidal curve was replaced with sharp, irregular, jittery fluctuations across all sample points.
- **Can you still recognize the original signal?**  
  **Yes.** Because the noise variance is moderate relative to signal amplitude, the underlying 5 Hz periodic sinusoidal trend remains clearly discernible.
- **Real-world source of signal noise:**  
  **Thermal noise (Johnson–Nyquist noise)** generated by the random thermal agitation of charge carriers inside electronic components (resistors, amplifiers, radio receivers).
